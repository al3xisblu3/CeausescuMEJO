Before the crowds filled the streets, Romania had endured years of political repression and economic hardship.

Power concentrated at the top

Nicolae Ceaușescu became the Communist Party’s leader in 1965. Early hopes for greater openness faded as he tightened control. Members of his family gained powerful positions, including his wife, Elena.

The cost of austerity

During the 1980s, Ceaușescu pushed Romania to repay its foreign debt. Imports and electricity use were cut while goods were directed toward export. These policies placed a heavy burden on ordinary people.

As communist governments elsewhere in Eastern Europe loosened their grip, Ceaușescu resisted reform. By late 1989, the distance between the government’s claims and people’s daily lives had become a central weakness of his rule.
