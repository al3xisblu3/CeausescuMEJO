1989

The Revolution

AI disclosure: The article text on this page was generated with assistance from OpenAI ChatGPT for MEJO 187 Project 2.

A protest over a pastor’s threatened eviction became a nationwide challenge to a dictatorship.

The uprising begins

In mid-December 1989, supporters gathered in Timișoara to defend pastor László Tőkés. The crowd grew, and its demands expanded into opposition to the government. On December 17, army and security forces fired on demonstrators. The violence failed to stop the protests.

A rally turns against the leader

On December 21, Ceaușescu addressed a rally in Bucharest intended to demonstrate support for his rule. Shouts from the crowd interrupted him. Television viewers saw a leader struggling to control an event organized to celebrate his authority.

Flight from Bucharest

Protests continued the next day. Nicolae and Elena Ceaușescu escaped from the Communist Party headquarters by helicopter. A new leadership emerged around Ion Iliescu and the National Salvation Front, but fighting continued after the couple’s departure.

The fall of the leader was only one part of the revolution. The struggle to establish a new government, and to understand the violence, continued beyond December.
